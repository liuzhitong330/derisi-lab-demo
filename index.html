The DeRisi team's MIS-C study connects an infection to an immune response against the body's own proteins. A practical question sits between a broad antibody screen and a follow-up experiment: which specimen calls are stable, and which depend on the control reference?

I built a small sensitivity explorer around the study's public PhIP-seq measurements. Change the screening cutoff, inspect individual specimens, and see whether removing just one control changes a call. The aim is to make follow-up choices inspectable, not to create a diagnostic test.

How it works

Each point is one published specimen. The source analysis aggregates peptide reads by gene and reports fold changes over mock immunoprecipitation. For a selected antigen, a reference line is the mean of the 45 at-risk controls plus k control standard deviations. The shaded band shows how that line moves when each control is omitted in turn.

This is a reanalysis of the discovery cohort: 199 MIS-C specimens and 45 controls. The three main antigens were already selected and validated in the paper. The additional UBE3A comparison illustrates why control behavior matters.

One follow-up question

Would specimens that are sensitive to the control reference also disagree with an orthogonal full-length protein assay? A small, balanced panel spanning the three groups could be informative. Check pretreatment status and specimen availability first; include appropriate binding and epitope controls. The paper already performed orthogonal validation, so this would be a targeted extension, not its first confirmation.

Why this matters for the lab

The work connects broad immunological screens to focused experiments. A transparent view of control dependence can help separate a reproducible screening call from a borderline one before committing additional specimens to follow-up. It does not establish cross-reactivity, pathogenicity, or a current internal bottleneck in the lab.

Methods note

The cutoff and leave-one-control-out procedure are an exploratory extension. A control z score is not a normal-theory p value, and the leave-one-out band is not a confidence interval. This is neither an independent validation cohort nor a reproduction of the paper's classifier.

Data, code and limitations

Data: Bodansky et al., Dryad (CC0). Research context: Nature, 2024. We retain processed gene-level fold changes without renormalizing them. Clinical treatment, batch and orthogonal-assay measurements are absent from these matrices; no IVIG adjustment or paired binding-assay analysis is performed. Findings may reflect cohort composition and treatment as well as biology. The paper's own untreated-subset and orthogonal-assay results remain separate evidence.

The lab-associated PhagePy repository (pinned commit) is linked from a separate DeRisi-team publication, not supplied as this MIS-C paper's official code. Its inspected control-based hit function is run on the compatible processed layer, with an independent numerical parity check. No upstream source is redistributed.
